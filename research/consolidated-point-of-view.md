# Consolidated point of view

## The human choice

Artificial intelligence is not only a new class of tool. It is a rapidly
expanding capacity to classify, persuade, coordinate, predict, create, and act.
That capacity enters the world through institutions that choose purposes,
control infrastructure, distribute gains, assign risk, and decide who may
contest an outcome.

The ethical question is therefore not whether an AI system can perform a task.
It is whether people and peoples retain the power to author the ends,
conditions, and limits of its use.

This is the consolidated view:

> **An AI transition is legitimate only when it protects intrinsic dignity,
> expands meaningful human agency, enables cooperation across difference,
> makes concentrations of power accountable, and preserves the ability to
> stop, revise, and repair.**

Those five requirements form **DAPPR: dignity, agency, plurality, power, and
reversibility**. DAPPR is the synthesis of this project. It is not attributed
to any one source.

The practical standard is demanding but simple: **society should remain the
author of the transition rather than become its audience.**

## Why these sources belong in one conversation

The source set is not a consensus. It is a productive argument among different
genres and moral languages:

- [Bill Gates](./source-briefs/01-gates-turbulent-ai-era.md) offers a public
  transition agenda centered on turbulence, equitable benefit, jobs, misuse,
  childhood, new institutions, Human Reserved work, and taxation.
- [*What Is Ethical AI?*](./source-briefs/02-what-is-ethical-ai.md) provides the
  institutional thesis: ethics governs power across the lifecycle; a model is
  not ethical by itself.
- [*Magnifica Humanitas*](./source-briefs/03-magnifica-humanitas.md) supplies
  the deepest anthropology and social doctrine: intrinsic dignity, common
  good, subsidiarity, solidarity, truth, work, education, relationship,
  future generations, and peace.
- [*Plurality*](./source-briefs/04-plurality-book-and-repository.md) provides a
  positive design direction: use digital systems to support collaboration
  across social difference.
- [RadicalxChange](./source-briefs/05-radicalxchange.md) turns part of that
  direction into mechanisms, experiments, and institutional proposals.
- [*AI 2027*](./source-briefs/07-ai-2027.md) makes a rapid-takeoff worldview
  specific enough to test and exposes how both catastrophe and a nominally
  good ending can concentrate power.
- [*AI 2040: Plan A*](./source-briefs/08-ai-2040-plan-a.md) proposes time,
  transparency, diffusion, and reversibility through an international
  arrangement while surfacing its own technical and political fragility.
- The [reading-group handout](./source-briefs/09-ai-2040-reading-group-handout.md)
  demonstrates a useful civic practice: organize discussion around cruxes,
  rival plans, failure modes, and resolvable forecasts.

Gates explicitly cites *Magnifica Humanitas* as a foundation. The other
relationships are primarily analytic: this project places the sources in
conversation without claiming that their authors have endorsed one another.

## Seven propositions

### 1. AI ethics is constitutional before it is computational

Accuracy, robustness, and alignment matter. They do not answer whether a
purpose is legitimate, whether a person can refuse, whether a public
institution should delegate its authority, or who receives remedy.

A perfectly accurate system can administer an unjust policy. A well-aligned
system can faithfully serve a concentrated or illegitimate principal. A
transparent system can expose people to surveillance. An efficient system can
make exclusion cheaper.

The first questions are therefore constitutional:

1. Who may set the objective?
2. What rights limit it?
3. Who participates before the objective is fixed?
4. Who controls the infrastructure?
5. Who must justify, monitor, and repair?
6. Which decisions remain human?
7. How can the arrangement be revised or ended?

Technical evaluation belongs inside this architecture, not in place of it.

### 2. Intelligence does not confer authority

Several sources are preoccupied with systems that may equal or exceed human
performance. Yet performance and legitimate authority are different
categories.

Gates's substitution argument depends on systems becoming sufficiently
reliable. *Magnifica Humanitas* contests the implied equivalence: persons are
embodied, relational, morally responsible, and capable of integrating error
into growth. *What Is Ethical AI?* adds that accuracy does not legitimate the
end. *AI 2040* confronts the issue most dramatically when a plan to preserve
human control culminates in "passing the torch."

The consolidated view is not that machines can never outperform people. It is
that **superior performance does not create a right to rule**. Advice,
execution, agenda setting, adjudication, coercion, care, and constitutional
change require different kinds of authorization. Some may be delegated under
conditions; others may carry non-delegable human responsibility.

### 3. Dignity becomes real through institutional capability

Dignity is often invoked as an inspiring preamble while optimization continues
unchanged. It becomes operational when a person has:

- rights that cannot be traded away for aggregate benefit;
- notice and intelligible reasons;
- the practical ability to challenge a decision;
- a human institution with authority to change it;
- remedy after harm;
- protection from manipulative or coercive dependence;
- recognition as a participant in setting ends.

This yields a compact formula:

> **Dignity without agency is ceremonial; agency without institutions is
> fragile.**

The formula is especially important in humanitarian, welfare, migration,
education, health, and workplace contexts where formal consent may conceal
dependency.

### 4. Participation must reach problem definition

Plurality is more than a larger comment box. People exercise authorship when
they can influence:

1. which problem receives resources;
2. how the problem is described;
3. which options exist;
4. what evidence counts;
5. how a decision is made;
6. who implements it;
7. how it is appealed and revised.

Quadratic voting and bridging systems can reveal intensity or cross-group
agreement. They do not make the option set just, solve identity and access, or
guarantee that final authority follows the result. Mechanism design must
therefore be paired with agenda accountability and power to act.

Plurality also qualifies universality. Equal dignity and rights are a floor;
they should not force every community into one social or technical form.
Difference can shape implementation, institutional imagination, and
collective learning without weakening the floor.

### 5. Work, truth, education, and relationship are governance infrastructure

The transition cannot be governed only at the frontier-model layer.

**Work** connects income to contribution, status, relationship, and public
revenue. If labor demand shifts, retraining by itself is insufficient. Society
must decide how productivity, time, ownership, taxation, bargaining power, and
social membership are reorganized.

**Truth** is not a content flag assigned by a central authority. It depends on
verification, source access, argument, journalism, trusted intermediaries, and
institutions able to correct themselves.

**Education** is not only preparation for jobs that remain. It develops the
capacity to ask questions, recognize manipulation, test a claim, live with
difference, and decide when not to delegate.

**Relationship** is not equivalent to frictionless responsiveness. Simulated
empathy can help, but reciprocal care includes obligation, vulnerability,
embodiment, disagreement, and responsibility.

These domains are where human agency is formed. Weakening them while promising
future abundance would make society less able to govern the abundance.

### 6. Scenario scrutiny is essential - and narrative humility is part of it

*AI 2027* and *AI 2040* perform a valuable service: they replace vague claims
with dates, actors, mechanisms, and failure branches. A proposal that cannot
be narrated causally is not yet a plan.

But a detailed scenario can create an illusion of inevitability. Good scenario
practice therefore requires:

- explicit separation of observed fact, source claim, scenario,
  recommendation, and synthesis;
- assumptions with evidence and counterevidence;
- alternate actors and political coalitions;
- a record of revisions;
- leading indicators and resolution dates;
- multiple capability timelines;
- attention to legitimacy, not only technical feasibility;
- a failure branch in which the proposed governor becomes a new concentration
  of power.

The right lesson is **use the method without inheriting the conclusion**.

### 7. Reversibility is a moral and political capability

Reversibility is more than a kill switch. It includes:

- the ability to delay deployment;
- bounded pilots and staged authorization;
- monitoring tied to action thresholds;
- expiration and renewal;
- correction of data and classification;
- appeal and compensation;
- retirement and deletion;
- institutional learning;
- the option not to automate;
- the ability of future publics to revise inherited arrangements.

Plan A makes reversibility explicit at the geopolitical level. The same idea
belongs in ordinary procurement, schools, hospitals, benefits systems, and
workplaces.

An arrangement that cannot be changed after error gives current designers
power over future people. Reversibility is therefore a way of respecting both
uncertainty and future agency.

## DAPPR in practice

### Dignity

**Test:** Is anyone treated only as an input, target, score, source of data, or
cost?

**Minimum evidence**

- protected rights and unacceptable uses;
- assessment of people who cannot meaningfully consent;
- treatment of embodiment, care, and relationship;
- remedy proportionate to the stakes;
- supply-chain and labor conditions.

**Failure mode:** dignity language appears in a preamble but no actor can stop
or repair the system.

### Agency

**Test:** Can affected people understand, refuse, contest, alter, and exit?

**Minimum evidence**

- intelligible notice and reasons;
- refusal without loss of an essential good where feasible;
- a reviewer with authority, time, and protection;
- accessible appeal;
- resources that make participation practical;
- preservation of human skill and judgment.

**Failure mode:** a powerless person remains "in the loop" and absorbs blame.

### Plurality

**Test:** Can people cooperate across difference without forced sameness?

**Minimum evidence**

- multiple publics involved before problem framing closes;
- visibility of minority-intense needs;
- language, disability, cultural, and regional access;
- explanation of agenda construction and moderation;
- legitimate disagreement retained in the record.

**Failure mode:** a single aggregate score turns disagreement into a
measurement error.

### Power

**Test:** Who sets the end, owns the stack, captures gains, and bears
responsibility?

**Minimum evidence**

- decision-rights and ownership map;
- incentive and resource flows;
- independent oversight with access and enforcement;
- labor and community bargaining power;
- public technical capacity;
- conflicts of interest;
- liability and duty bearers.

**Failure mode:** governance constrains a model while leaving procurement,
capital, and platform power untouched.

### Reversibility

**Test:** What can still be stopped, changed, repaired, or withdrawn?

**Minimum evidence**

- pre-deployment gates;
- monitored thresholds;
- rollback and service continuity;
- sunset dates;
- incident response and compensation;
- retirement and data disposition;
- a process for future communities to change the rules.

**Failure mode:** "pilot" becomes permanent before evidence is reviewed.

## Where the sources converge

### Capability does not distribute benefit automatically

Gates makes the distributional argument directly. *Magnifica Humanitas*
subjects digital goods and infrastructure to the common good. *Plurality*
builds counter-institutions. *AI 2040* fears both extinction and winner-take-all
power. The shared conclusion is institutional: abundance does not govern its
own distribution.

### Concentration is a primary risk

The encyclical examines private de facto government; Gates worries about
benefits accruing to a small group; Plurality opposes centralized technocracy;
both AI Futures scenarios show frontier labs and state executives making
civilizational decisions.

### Existing institutions are inadequate

The sources disagree on what to build, not on whether current arrangements are
sufficient. Proposals include new national and international bodies, lifecycle
accountability, subsidiarity, plural digital infrastructure, frontier compute
verification, and public deliberation.

### Human development matters

Work, education, critical thought, care, relationship, participation, and
meaning are not externalities. They are part of what the technology is for.

### Public choice must become more intentional

Each source resists passive spectatorship. Even *AI 2027*, the least
prescriptive, invites counter-scenarios and public evaluation.

## Where they disagree

### Is advanced cognition substitution or imitation?

- **Gates:** sufficiently reliable AI can replace and exceed human cognition.
- ***Magnifica Humanitas*:** computational capability imitates selected
  functions but lacks the embodied, relational, moral, and spiritual reality
  of a person.
- **Synthesis:** resolve task performance empirically; refuse to infer moral
  standing or authority from performance alone.

### Can the frontier be slowed?

- **Gates:** would support a credible global slowdown but considers incentives
  too strong.
- **Plan A:** treats an international slowdown as the central recommendation.
- ***Plurality*:** frames the choice in its conclusion as one of direction more
  than velocity.
- **Synthesis:** pursue no-regrets governance under several timelines while
  investing in verification and diplomacy that can expand the feasible set.

### How open should knowledge be?

- ***Plurality*:** openness and protocols support collaboration and public
  value.
- **Plan A:** total research transparency is both verification and diffusion.
- **Safety critique:** open algorithms can aid defectors and actors with the
  most compute.
- **Synthesis:** replace "open versus closed" with tiered access, public
  accountability, researcher access, privacy, security, and reciprocal
  obligations.

### At what scale should authority sit?

- **Plan A:** high-stakes coordination begins with the two largest powers and
  frontier compute.
- ***Magnifica Humanitas*:** subsidiarity and solidarity must interlock across
  levels.
- ***Plurality*:** overlapping publics and adaptive administration should
  shape decisions.
- **Synthesis:** use the largest scale necessary for cross-border risk and the
  smallest capable scale for situated choice, connected by rights, evidence,
  and appeal.

### What is the future of work?

- **Gates:** significant permanent displacement; reserve some work and tax
  machine production.
- **Plan A:** radical abundance, permits, and dividends.
- ***Magnifica Humanitas*:** work is participation and dignity, so assistance
  alone cannot be the response.
- **Synthesis:** separate income, bargaining power, contribution, care,
  identity, and community; design institutions for each rather than expecting
  one transfer mechanism to replace employment.

### Should humanity ever hand off?

Plan A imagines a trustworthy handoff. The dignity and plurality traditions
suggest that some authority remains human because legitimacy arises through
persons and peoples, not capability rank.

The burden of proof should therefore be reversed: name each power proposed for
delegation and justify it. "The AI is wiser" is not sufficient authorization.

## A no-regrets institutional agenda

These actions remain useful under slow, medium, and fast capability progress.

### In the next 12-24 months

1. **Create public purpose registers.** Consequential deployments should state
   the problem, legitimate authority, affected groups, alternatives, expected
   benefit, rights at risk, and exit conditions.
2. **Name lifecycle duty bearers.** Assign accountability from problem
   selection through retirement; do not let vendors and deployers point at
   one another.
3. **Guarantee contest and remedy.** Build notice, human review with authority,
   correction, appeal, incident reporting, and compensation appropriate to
   stakes.
4. **Fund affected participation.** Pay for time, expertise, translation,
   accessibility, community governance, and independent advice before
   procurement is fixed.
5. **Establish forecast registers.** Record high-impact assumptions,
   probabilities, resolution dates, and update rules.
6. **Use bounded authorization.** Stage deployment, monitor actual benefit,
   and require renewal rather than allowing pilots to become permanent.

### In the next two to five years

7. **Build public technical capacity.** Regulators, schools, health systems,
   international organizations, and civil society need independent expertise
   and infrastructure.
8. **Negotiate transition compacts.** Workers and communities should share
   productivity gains, influence deployment pace, and shape retraining,
   shorter work, taxation, ownership, and local investment.
9. **Deliberate Human Reserved authority.** Identify care, coercive,
   adjudicative, educational, and civic decisions where human presence or
   responsibility remains essential.
10. **Invest in plural digital public goods.** Support open protocols,
    deliberation, identity protections, data institutions, and mechanisms that
    distribute agenda power.
11. **Develop verifiable capability governance.** Fund compute accounting,
    secure evaluation, incident-sharing, and treaty verification without
    assuming that compute is a permanent proxy for capability.
12. **Connect rights floors across borders.** Use UN processes to align notice,
    remedy, safety reporting, scientific evidence, and participation while
    preserving plural implementation.

### Before any extraordinary delegation

13. **Define non-delegable authority.** Constitutional change, lethal force,
    deprivation of rights, and other high-stakes powers require explicit
    public rules.
14. **Require a legitimacy case beside the safety case.** Technical control is
    necessary but does not show that the principal, purpose, or distribution
    is just.
15. **Preserve future consent.** No generation should make an irreversible
    handoff merely because a system passes tests designed by the present
    governors.

## The teaching point of view

An ethical technology course should not graduate students who can recite
principles but cannot locate a decision owner.

Students should practice:

- source verification;
- moral reasoning;
- power mapping;
- lifecycle governance;
- participatory design;
- institutional economics;
- scenario scrutiny;
- public explanation;
- designing stop and repair mechanisms.

The course should also protect forms of learning that instant generation can
weaken: sustained reading, independent judgment, dialogue, revision,
uncertainty, and encounter with people who do not answer on demand.

The goal is not to make students anti-AI. It is to make them capable of
choosing when, why, how, and under whose authority AI should be used.

## Audience translations

### For the AI-2040 meeting

> Plan A's greatest contribution is scenario scrutiny and its insistence on
> time, visibility, diffusion, and reversibility. Its unresolved layer is
> legitimacy: a verifiable bargain among great powers is not yet a polity, and
> a plan for human control should not end in an undefined transfer of human
> authority.

### For a salon

> The practical divide is not optimism versus pessimism. It is passive
> inevitability versus institutional authorship. What should we enable, what
> should we reserve, who decides, and what must remain reversible?

### For UNGA and human-dignity forums

> Human dignity must become decision power: a rights floor, meaningful
> participation, public capacity, contestability, remedy, and institutions
> that prevent both private and state technological power from becoming
> unanswerable.

### For the Ethical Tech CoLab

> Move from principle lists to institutional prototypes. Every student project
> should name a legitimate purpose, affected participants, decision rights,
> evidence, duty bearers, appeal, repair, and stop conditions.

## Claims to resist

1. **"AI is inevitable."** Capabilities may be difficult to coordinate;
   purposes, deployments, ownership, and institutions are choices.
2. **"A human is in the loop."** Ask what that person can see, decide, stop,
   and survive professionally.
3. **"The model is aligned."** Ask with whom, to what purpose, through which
   legitimate process, and with what contest.
4. **"The system is accurate."** Ask whether the objective and institution are
   just.
5. **"The process was participatory."** Ask what participants could change.
6. **"The research is open."** Ask who can use it, who is exposed, and what
   reciprocal duties apply.
7. **"The benefits will be shared."** Ask through which ownership, bargaining,
   fiscal, and public-service institutions.
8. **"We can hand off when it is smarter."** Ask which authority intelligence
   could ever legitimately confer.

## Twelve propositions for discussion

1. The main alignment problem is not only aligning machines to values; it is
   aligning institutions that choose and enforce those values.
2. Accuracy is evidence about means, never a verdict on ends.
3. A technically safe system can still be politically illegitimate.
4. Consent is weakest where a system matters most.
5. Participation after procurement is often too late.
6. The right not to automate is part of democratic technology policy.
7. Work policy must protect agency and membership, not only income.
8. Truth requires institutions for verification and correction, not one
   machine authorized to declare it.
9. Openness without capacity may widen power gaps.
10. A global agreement that excludes most of the world can be stable only by
    accident or coercion.
11. Reversibility is how uncertain institutions respect future people.
12. The success condition is not human control in the abstract; it is people
    and peoples retaining authorship of their common life.
