# Executive brief

## The view in one paragraph

The central issue across the source set is not machine intelligence in
isolation. It is power: who chooses purposes, controls infrastructure,
receives benefit, absorbs risk, and can contest or reverse a decision.
Legitimate AI governance should protect intrinsic dignity, expand meaningful
agency, support cooperation across difference, make concentrated power
answerable, and preserve the ability to stop and repair. This project's name
for those five tests is **DAPPR: dignity, agency, plurality, power, and
reversibility**. The success condition is not merely "human control" in the
abstract. It is people and peoples retaining authorship of their common life.

## Five conclusions

### 1. Ethical AI is an institutional achievement

Accuracy, robustness, transparency, and technical alignment are necessary in
many settings. They do not establish that a purpose is legitimate or an
institution is just. Accountability must begin with problem selection and run
through retirement, appeal, and remedy.

### 2. Intelligence does not confer authority

Systems may outperform people at many tasks. Performance does not create a
right to set ends, adjudicate rights, exercise coercion, substitute for
reciprocal care, or amend the rules under which people live.

### 3. Dignity must become decision power

Dignity is operational when people have protected rights, meaningful voice,
notice, contestability, a reviewer with actual authority, and repair after
harm. Dignity without agency is ceremonial; agency without capable
institutions is fragile.

### 4. Participation must reach the agenda

Plural and quadratic mechanisms can reveal intensity and cross-group support.
They do not decide who frames the problem, chooses the options, controls
identity infrastructure, or honors the result. Participation should influence
ends, evidence, decision rights, and remedy.

### 5. Uncertainty demands scenarios and reversibility

*AI 2027* and *AI 2040* show the value of a detailed causal story. They also
show the risk of narrative certainty. Institutions should record assumptions,
dates, and disconfirming evidence while building pauses, sunset clauses,
rollback, appeal, and future revision into ordinary and frontier governance.

## Five tensions to keep alive

1. **Substitution:** Gates expects reliable cognition to replace people;
   *Magnifica Humanitas* argues human intelligence is embodied, relational,
   fallible, morally responsible, and different in kind.
2. **Pace:** Gates sees global slowdown as implausible; Plan A makes it the
   recommendation; *Plurality* frames the choice as one of direction more than
   velocity in its conclusion.
3. **Openness:** public knowledge can distribute power and support inspection;
   it can also advantage defectors and actors with the most compute.
4. **Scale:** global coordination is necessary, but a US-China frontier bargain
   does not equal global legitimacy.
5. **Handoff:** Plan A seeks human control and ends with a transfer to trusted
   systems. A technical confidence threshold is not a constitutional
   authorization.

## What to say in each room

### AI-2040 meeting

> Steal the method, not necessarily the conclusion. Plan A's scenario scrutiny
> is valuable; its missing layer is legitimacy. Verification among great
> powers does not tell us who may set the threshold, whose security counts, or
> what authority humanity may never hand off.

### Ethical Tech CoLab

> Move from principle lists to institutional prototypes. Every project should
> name a legitimate purpose, affected participants, decision rights, evidence,
> duty bearers, appeal, remedy, and stop condition.

### Salon

> The useful divide is not optimism versus pessimism. It is passive
> inevitability versus institutional authorship: what should we enable, what
> should we reserve for people, who decides, and what must remain reversible?

### UNGA / human dignity

> Human dignity must become decision power: a rights floor, meaningful
> participation, public capacity, contestability, remedy, and multilateral
> institutions in which countries and communities outside the frontier are
> authors rather than rule-takers.
