# Power and the Human Person

## An institutional introduction to ethical AI

**Status:** sourcebook blueprint for review
**Proposed length:** 35,000-45,000 words
**Course fit:** weeks 1-6, 9-10, and 13-14
**Primary spine:** *What Is Ethical AI?* and *Magnifica Humanitas*, with the
Gates essay as a contemporary transition case

## Promise to the reader

This book begins before principles. It asks why societies create ethical and
legal institutions when capability grows, how AI redistributes authority, and
what human dignity requires when systems can predict, persuade, and act at
scale.

Its thesis is institutional:

> AI is not made ethical by adding a moral feature to a model. It becomes
> governable when legitimate purposes, rights, participation, duty bearers,
> evidence, limits, contest, and remedy shape its entire lifecycle.

The book is designed for readers in policy, international affairs,
humanitarian practice, public-interest technology, education, philanthropy,
and responsible innovation. It assumes no machine-learning background.

## Chapter sequence

### 1. The capability paradox

**Question:** Why does every increase in power expand both possibility and
capacity for harm?

**Argument:** AI belongs to a recurring institutional problem rather than a
wholly unprecedented moral universe. Writing required law, industrialization
required labor and public-health institutions, and nuclear capability required
international regimes. The novelty lies in the type, distribution, and speed
of power.

**Primary pathway**

- [*What Is Ethical AI?*, executive summary](https://yorkerhodes3.github.io/pageturn-book/book/what-is-ethical-ai/2026-07/chapters/executive-summary/)
- ["Humanity's Relationship with Power"](https://yorkerhodes3.github.io/pageturn-book/book/what-is-ethical-ai/2026-07/chapters/power/)
- [Gates, opening and "This time really is different"](https://www.gatesnotes.com/work/make-ai-work-for-everyone/reader/a-turbulent-ai-era-and-critical-choices-to-make?WT.mc_id=20260826_ai-overture-2026-med-med)

**Studio:** Draw the power system around one AI use. Include infrastructure,
capital, data, labor, standards, procurement, affected non-users, and remedy.

### 2. Ethics, law, and legitimacy

**Question:** What does ethics contribute that legality, market choice, and
technical performance do not?

**Argument:** Ethics supplies the normative architecture for choosing and
limiting ends. Law gives some judgments institutional form; neither current
law nor voluntary principles exhaust legitimacy.

**Concepts:** consequences, duties, rights, virtues, care, capability,
universalism without uniformity, ethical realism.

**Studio:** Submit one case to five moral traditions. Identify whether each
disagreement is about value, fact, institutional competence, or strategy.

### 3. Dignity before optimization

**Question:** What may not be traded away even for a large aggregate benefit?

**Argument:** Dignity means a person cannot be reduced to a score, data source,
target, cost, or instrument. Human rights are an institutional floor. The
Christian anthropology of *Magnifica Humanitas* goes further: embodiment,
relationship, conscience, freedom, fallibility, grace, and responsibility
make a person more than a performance profile.

**Primary pathway**

- [*Magnifica Humanitas*](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html),
  §§57-84, 97-110, 125-130.
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights, preamble and selected articles.

**Studio:** Identify a non-compensable dignity floor and defend it against a
better aggregate outcome.

### 4. Accuracy is not ethics

**Question:** Why can a flawless system remain wrong?

**Argument:** Performance establishes that a means works. It does not establish
that the objective, institution, or distribution is legitimate. Human review
does not solve the problem if the reviewer lacks authority, information,
time, or protection.

**Cases:** benefits eligibility, predictive policing, medical triage,
automated dismissal, humanitarian targeting.

**Studio:** Rewrite a model evaluation as an institutional authorization case.
Add purpose, authority, rights, alternatives, duty bearers, appeal, and stop
conditions.

### 5. The full lifecycle of responsibility

**Question:** Where does accountability live when decisions cross funders,
vendors, deployers, professionals, and public authorities?

**Argument:** Responsibility begins at problem selection and continues through
data, design, evaluation, procurement, deployment, monitoring, incident
response, remedy, retirement, and data disposition. Accountability requires a
person or institution able to justify, monitor, change, and repair.

**Studio:** Build a lifecycle map in which every decision has one accountable
owner and one route for affected people to challenge it.

### 6. Consent where refusal is impossible

**Question:** What becomes of consent in crisis, poverty, dependency, or
essential public services?

**Argument:** Humanitarian AI reveals a general limit of consumer ethics.
People may have no meaningful alternative, no vote over the deployer, and no
purchasing power. Experimentation reaches the least protected while
accountability fragments across institutions.

**Primary pathway**

- ["The Historical Evolution of AI in Humanitarian Work"](https://yorkerhodes3.github.io/pageturn-book/book/what-is-ethical-ai/2026-07/chapters/humanitarian-history/)
- ["Why Humanitarian AI Is Fundamentally Different"](https://yorkerhodes3.github.io/pageturn-book/book/what-is-ethical-ai/2026-07/chapters/humanitarian-difference/)

**Studio:** Write the future incident report and design the gate that could
have prevented it.

### 7. Work, care, and Human Reserved

**Question:** What should remain substantially human even if substitution is
technically possible?

**Argument:** Work is income, but also participation, contribution,
relationship, recognition, skill, and a tax base. Gates's Human Reserved
proposal opens a democratic question about non-substitution. It must be
designed to protect vulnerable people and non-delegable responsibility rather
than preserve status for insiders.

**Primary pathway**

- Gates, "Set aside some jobs for humans" and tax proposal.
- *Magnifica Humanitas*, §§148-169.
- *Power and Progress*, selected historical chapters.

**Studio:** Convene workers, care recipients, employers, government, and
people outside formal work to define one reserved activity and a review
process.

### 8. Education, truth, and relationship

**Question:** What capacities does a society need in order to govern powerful
systems?

**Argument:** Critical thought, source verification, sustained inquiry,
dialogue, embodied presence, and reciprocal care are governance capacity.
Education should teach not only how to use AI but when and why not to use it.

**Primary pathway**

- *Magnifica Humanitas*, §§132-147 and 170-172.
- Gates, sections on childhood, companions, education, and productive
  struggle.

**Studio:** Compare an AI-generated answer, an independently reasoned answer,
and a dialogic revision. Name the human capability each method develops.

### 9. From dignity to institutional power

**Question:** How does a moral commitment survive contact with budgets,
procurement, incentives, and enforcement?

**Argument:** Dignity becomes decision power through rights, early
participation, capable public institutions, independent oversight, appeal,
remedy, and reversibility. A preamble without these mechanisms is ceremonial.

**Studio:** Draft an institutional charter with purpose, protected floor,
decision rights, evidence, enforcement, resources, and sunset.

### 10. The human choice

**Question:** What should remain true even if capability forecasts are wrong?

**Argument:** Intelligence does not confer authority. Society should retain
authorship of ends and constitutional limits under slow and fast futures.

**Final artifact:** a domain-specific constitution for an AI-mediated
institution, tested for dignity, agency, concentrated power, plural
participation, and reversibility.

## Reusable chapter apparatus

Every chapter should contain:

1. one primary-source excerpt under 120 words;
2. an epistemic-label exercise;
3. a case from outside the technology sector;
4. an affected-person perspective;
5. an institution and power map;
6. a "what would change my view?" box;
7. a design studio;
8. a short counter-reading.
