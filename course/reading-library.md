# Reading library and source pathways

This is a teaching library, not a claim that every text should be assigned in
full. The "use" column identifies the work a reading can do in the semester.
Publication metadata should be checked against the edition selected by the
instructor.

## Core shelf: power, institutions, and justice

### *Atlas of AI*

**Kate Crawford (2021)**

Use to move analysis from a seemingly immaterial model to extraction, labor,
classification, infrastructure, energy, and political power. Pair with the
dataset genealogy in week 5.

### *Power and Progress: Our Thousand-Year Struggle Over Technology and
Prosperity*

**Daron Acemoglu and Simon Johnson (2023)**

Use against technological inevitability. Its historical argument that
productivity gains do not automatically become broad prosperity provides a
direct counterweight and extension to Gates's transition concerns.

### *The Black Box Society*

**Frank Pasquale (2015)**

Use for opacity as an institutional arrangement rather than only a technical
property. Strong for the transition from explainability to accountability.

### *Automating Inequality*

**Virginia Eubanks (2018)**

Use for grounded cases in which automated administration reaches people with
the least practical ability to refuse. Pair with humanitarian asymmetry and
the Dignity & Agency Audit.

### *Race After Technology*

**Ruha Benjamin (2019)**

Use to examine how formally neutral systems can deepen inequity and how
"innovation" can obscure discriminatory design. Pair with classification and
institutional imagination.

### *Algorithms of Oppression*

**Safiya Umoja Noble (2018)**

Use for commercial information systems, ranking, representation, and the
relationship between business models and discriminatory outcomes.

### *Unmasking AI*

**Joy Buolamwini (2023)**

Use as a first-person bridge among technical evaluation, public advocacy,
institutional resistance, and policy change.

## Core shelf: data, design, and participation

### [*Data Feminism*](https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/)

**Catherine D'Ignazio and Lauren F. Klein (2020)**

Use its principles to examine power, challenge binaries and hierarchies,
elevate emotion and embodiment, rethink labor, and embrace pluralism. The open
edition supports accessible course use.

### [*Design Justice*](https://design-justice.pubpub.org/)

**Sasha Costanza-Chock (2020)**

Use to test the difference between consulting affected people and shifting
design power. Pair with lifecycle governance and final constitutions.

### *Justice by Means of Democracy*

**Danielle Allen (2023)**

Use for democracy as a route to justice, political equality, and connected
citizenship. It supplies a deeper normative foundation for the Plurality
weeks.

### *The Ethical Algorithm*

**Michael Kearns and Aaron Roth (2019)**

Use selectively to show what can be formalized - privacy, fairness, and
strategic behavior - and what remains an institutional and political choice.

### *Weapons of Math Destruction*

**Cathy O'Neil (2016)**

Use early for a shared vocabulary of opacity, scale, and damage. Later, ask
students which critiques need updating for generative and agentic systems.

## Core shelf: intelligence, control, and human meaning

### *Human Compatible*

**Stuart Russell (2019)**

Use for the control problem and uncertainty about human preferences. Pair with
the anthropological claims in *Magnifica Humanitas* and ask whether preference
learning is sufficient for dignity.

### *The Alignment Problem*

**Brian Christian (2020)**

Use for the history of technical alignment, reward design, imitation, and
human values. It helps students connect technical methods to institutional
choices.

### *AI Ethics*

**Mark Coeckelbergh (2020)**

Use as a concise conceptual map for agency, responsibility, consciousness,
policy, and philosophical traditions.

### *Technology and the Virtues*

**Shannon Vallor (2016)**

Use to ask what kind of people and communities technologies help form, not
only what discrete outcomes they produce.

### *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*

**Melanie Mitchell (2019)**

Use to discipline claims about intelligence and generality. Pair with both AI
Futures scenarios and *Magnifica Humanitas* paragraph 99.

### *AI Snake Oil*

**Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor (2024)**

Use as a structured skeptical counterpoint on prediction, generative AI, and
the gap between demos and dependable systems.

## Strategic counterpoints

### *The Coming Wave*

**Mustafa Suleyman with Michael Bhaskar (2023)**

Use for the containment dilemma and the argument that powerful technologies
become widely available. Compare its state-capacity proposals with Plan A's
international deal.

### *Resisting AI*

**Dan McQuillan (2022)**

Use to challenge reformist governance and ask whether some AI institutions
reproduce authoritarian and austerity logics too deeply to be repaired by
principles.

### *More Than a Glitch*

**Meredith Broussard (2023)**

Use to resist treating bias as an isolated error and to examine structural
limits on what automated systems should decide.

### *The Anxious Generation*

**Jonathan Haidt (2024)**

Gates cites this work in his discussion of childhood and AI companionship.
Use with critical empirical responses rather than as a settled account.

rights with operational language such as risk, measurement, management, and
assurance.

## Four reading pathways

### Path A - Foundations without technical prerequisites

1. *What Is Ethical AI?*
2. Coeckelbergh, *AI Ethics*
3. *Magnifica Humanitas*
4. *Data Feminism*
5. *Plurality*

**Best for:** policy, humanitarian, international affairs, and humanities
students.

### Path B - Power and accountability

1. *Atlas of AI*
2. *The Black Box Society*
3. *Automating Inequality*
4. *Power and Progress*
5. *Design Justice*

**Best for:** students studying institutions, political economy, procurement,
or public-interest technology.

### Path C - Futures and control

1. Mitchell, *A Guide for Thinking Humans*
2. Russell, *Human Compatible*
3. *AI 2027*
4. *The Coming Wave*
5. *AI 2040*

**Best for:** scenario analysis and AI-governance students. Require explicit
epistemic labels throughout.

### Path D - Democracy and collective intelligence

1. Allen, *Justice by Means of Democracy*
2. *Plurality*
3. *Design Justice*
4. RadicalxChange mechanism notes and critiques
5. UN Global Dialogue materials

**Best for:** the participatory design studio and students building public
institutions.

## Pairings that create productive disagreement

| Pair | Productive tension |
| --- | --- |
| Gates + *Power and Progress* | Is broad benefit a likely result of innovation or an institutional achievement? |
| *Magnifica Humanitas* + Russell | Is preference satisfaction an adequate account of the human good? |
| *Plurality* + *Design Justice* | Does better collaboration redistribute decision power far enough? |
| *AI 2027* + *AI Snake Oil* | Which capability claims are extrapolation, and which are grounded? |
| *AI 2040* + Global Digital Compact | Can a great-power bargain be globally legitimate? |
| *The Ethical Algorithm* + *The Black Box Society* | Which ethical problems can be formalized, and who decides the objective? |
| *Data Feminism* + Plan A | What knowledge and people disappear in compute-centered governance? |
| *Magnifica Humanitas* + *Plurality* | How do universal dignity and plural difference constrain one another? |

## Selection rule

Assign fewer whole books and more purposeful pairings. Every reading should
answer a named question, supply a method, present a case, or challenge a course
assumption. If it does none of these, it belongs in the library rather than on
the required list.
