# Assignments, studio protocols, and demonstrations

## 1. Claim / evidence / action cards

**Duration:** weekly, 20-30 minutes
**Output:** one page

Students select one consequential sentence from the week's reading and record:

1. exact quotation and anchor;
2. epistemic label;
3. strongest evidence offered;
4. strongest omitted counterevidence;
5. institution expected to act;
6. action proposed or implied;
7. observation that would change the student's view.

The exercise prevents a seminar from turning a vivid claim into an unmarked
fact.

## 2. Full-lifecycle accountability map

**Duration:** two weeks
**Output:** visual map plus 1,200-word memo

Map:

- problem definition;
- funding and procurement;
- data creation and rights;
- model development and evaluation;
- integration and user interface;
- deployment decision;
- monitoring and incident response;
- appeal and remedy;
- retirement and data disposition.

At each stage name the decision owner, affected parties, required evidence,
non-delegable human judgment, and stop condition. Award more credit for finding
gaps than for making the diagram look complete.

## 3. Human dignity impact memorandum

**Duration:** three weeks
**Output:** 1,800 words

Students assess a proposed AI deployment through:

- intrinsic dignity and non-instrumental treatment;
- rights as a floor;
- agency, refusal, and appeal;
- distribution of benefit and burden;
- effect on work, relationship, and social membership;
- participation of affected communities;
- remedy after foreseeable and unanticipated harm.

The conclusion must be one of: proceed, proceed with conditions, redesign,
pause pending evidence, or do not deploy. "Proceed responsibly" is not a
decision.

## 4. Plural governance prototype

**Duration:** three weeks
**Output:** tested mechanism and design record

Teams choose a real collective decision and prototype one mechanism: augmented
deliberation, plural voting, participatory budgeting, citizen assembly,
community jury, or another justified design.

Required tests:

1. change who sets the agenda;
2. introduce a minority-intense preference;
3. add an identity or access failure;
4. reveal a privacy tradeoff;
5. replay the decision after participants receive new evidence.

The prototype is graded on what the team learns about power, not whether the
mechanism produces consensus.

## 5. Scenario scrutiny portfolio

**Duration:** two weeks
**Output:** a two-branch timeline and assumption ledger

For a chosen policy:

1. write the dated path by which it succeeds;
2. identify the first step that feels forced;
3. write the most plausible failure branch;
4. add leading indicators and resolution dates;
5. identify second-order effects and new concentrations of power;
6. revise the proposal to survive one failure;
7. explain which moral commitments remain even if the timeline is wrong.

This exercise takes scenario scrutiny from *AI 2040* while refusing to treat
the source's preferred scenario as a fact.

## 6. Final: constitution for an AI-mediated institution

**Duration:** final four weeks
**Output:** public artifact, defense, and revision log

Teams write a short constitution for an institution in health, education,
humanitarian response, public benefits, work, media, or civic participation.

Every article must include:

- protected value or right;
- duty bearer;
- evidence required;
- decision and enforcement power;
- participation mechanism;
- contest and remedy;
- review date;
- emergency pause and permanent exit conditions.

The constitution must pass:

- a dignity challenge from a person who cannot meaningfully consent;
- a plurality challenge from a community that rejects the majority framing;
- a power challenge from an actor who controls critical infrastructure;
- a reversibility challenge after a severe incident;
- a futures challenge under both slow and fast capability progress.

## Demonstration protocols

### Scenario Scrutiny Canvas

Use the linked browser exercise to create a policy timeline. Run it in this
order:

1. Write the desired 2030 or 2040 endpoint.
2. Work backward to the first decision that must occur.
3. Assign an actor, incentive, evidence threshold, and enforcement mechanism
   to each step.
4. Duplicate the first step that depends on cooperation and write a defection
   branch.
5. Export or print the result.

**Debrief:** Which sentence changed from "then" to an actual causal mechanism?

### Plural Voice Lab

Every participant receives an equal voice-credit budget. Additional votes on
one option cost quadratically.

Run three rounds:

1. supplied options and anonymous preferences;
2. participants can propose options;
3. identities and reasons are discussed before reallocation.

**Debrief:** Did the mechanism reveal intensity, create legitimacy, or merely
produce a different tally? Who could not use it safely?

### Dignity & Agency Audit

Teams answer concrete questions across DAPPR. Require a note and evidence link
for every high score.

**Debrief:** Which criterion should be a non-compensable floor? Which score
tempted the team to convert a moral judgment into false precision?

## AI-use protocol for student work

AI assistance is permitted when it expands inquiry and documented when it
shapes the output.

Students must:

- preserve prompts or a concise interaction log;
- verify source claims at the linked primary source;
- mark generated hypotheses as hypotheses;
- name substantive changes they made;
- disclose when an AI tool generated code, prose, images, or analysis;
- avoid entering sensitive or non-public community data;
- never substitute simulated stakeholder responses for participation by
  affected people.

Some sessions begin with a short no-AI interval. The purpose is not purity; it
is to notice which capacities grow only through patient attention, dialogue,
and independent judgment.

## Rubric shared across assignments

| Dimension | Excellent evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Epistemic discipline | Claims are typed correctly; uncertainty and disconfirmation are explicit |
| Power analysis | Decision rights, incentives, infrastructure, and missing actors are visible |
| Dignity and rights | Non-negotiable floors are distinguished from tradeoffs |
| Participation | Affected people influence ends, not only implementation details |
| Institutional fit | Duties have capable owners, resources, enforcement, and review |
| Reversibility | Stop, repair, appeal, sunset, and learning mechanisms are concrete |
| Constructive imagination | Critique produces a more governable alternative |
