# Salon kit: Who gets to author the AI transition?

## Purpose

This salon is designed to move a mixed group beyond the familiar binary of
acceleration versus prohibition. It asks participants to identify what should
be enabled, constrained, shared, reserved for people, and made reversible.

**Recommended length:** 90 minutes
**Ideal group:** 18-40 people at mixed tables of 4-6
**Desired output:** one institutional commitment and one unresolved crux per
table

## Invitation language

> Artificial intelligence is often described as an irresistible force. But
> technology arrives through choices about investment, design, ownership,
> labor, law, education, and public purpose. This salon asks a prior question:
> who gets to make those choices - and what must remain true of human dignity,
> agency, and democratic life as capabilities change?

## Opening provocation: five minutes

> Across very different sources, the argument is converging on power.
> Bill Gates says the transition could make AI either a great equalizer or a
> source of profound injustice and argues that current institutions are not
> prepared. *What Is Ethical AI?* says ethics is not a product feature:
> systems become ethical, or fail to, through institutions across their whole
> lifecycle. *Magnifica Humanitas* asks whether technology makes life more
> human and warns that private digital power can exceed the capacity of
> governments. *Plurality* asks whether technology can help us cooperate
> across difference rather than compress it. *AI 2027* and *AI 2040* make the
> future concrete enough to argue with.
>
> These sources do not agree on intelligence, timelines, openness, or the best
> institution. But they let us ask a common question: can we remain authors of
> the transition?

## Three provocations

### 1. AI ethics begins where product ethics ends

A model card cannot decide whether a purpose is legitimate, who should bear
risk, or whether an institution should automate a decision at all.

**Table question:** Which apparently technical decision in your domain is
actually a constitutional decision about power?

### 2. "Human Reserved" is a choice about goods, not only jobs

Gates proposes reserving some work for people even where substitution becomes
technically possible. The deeper question is what kind of encounter, judgment,
care, responsibility, or civic authority should not be optimized away.

**Table question:** Name one activity that should remain substantially human.
What is lost if a system performs it flawlessly?

### 3. Plurality is not more comments; it is shared authorship

Inviting feedback does not redistribute agenda power. Participation becomes
meaningful when people can shape the problem, options, evidence, decision, and
remedy.

**Table question:** Where in your institution can affected people actually
change the end being pursued?

## DAPPR table canvas

### Dignity

- Who could be reduced to a score, target, source of data, or cost?
- Which treatment is unacceptable even if aggregate outcomes improve?
- What is owed after harm?

### Agency

- Who can understand the arrangement?
- Who can refuse without losing an essential good?
- Who can override, contest, appeal, or leave?

### Plurality

- Who helped frame the problem?
- Which differences are preserved rather than averaged away?
- Can a minority-intense need alter the decision?

### Power

- Who sets the objective and owns infrastructure?
- Who captures productivity or status gains?
- Who carries legal, financial, and moral responsibility?

### Reversibility

- What signal pauses the system?
- Can a person recover from a bad classification?
- When does authorization expire, and who can withdraw it?

## Prompt deck

Use one card per table rather than asking every question.

1. **Perfectly accurate, wrong purpose:** Name a system that would remain
   unethical even at 100% accuracy.
2. **Consent under pressure:** When does clicking "agree" fail to authorize an
   AI use?
3. **The missing public:** Who is affected without being a user, buyer,
   employee, or voter?
4. **Private government:** Which platform decision has become equivalent to
   public rulemaking?
5. **The right to slowness:** Where would delay improve judgment rather than
   merely postpone a decision?
6. **The right to relation:** Which encounter requires reciprocal presence,
   not simulated empathy?
7. **Plural truth:** How can institutions support verification without giving
   one actor centralized control over truth?
8. **Future standing:** How should future generations be represented in a
   decision about infrastructure or irreversible capability?
9. **Open, but to whom?:** When does openness democratize power, and when does
   it advantage actors with compute and expertise?
10. **A new governor:** If a policy constrains frontier labs, what prevents the
    regulator, auditor, or permit issuer from becoming the next unaccountable
    center?
11. **Meaning after work:** Which institutions provide contribution,
    recognition, and belonging if employment declines?
12. **The handoff:** What decision should no society delegate, however
    impressive an AI's judgment becomes?

## Productive tension cards

### Substitution

- **Gates:** AI can replace and exceed human cognition.
- **Magnifica Humanitas §99:** machine and human intelligence should not be
  equated; human experience, embodiment, relationship, conscience, and
  responsibility are categorically different.
- **Salon task:** decide whether this is an empirical disagreement, a
  definitional disagreement, or two questions passing one another.

### Pace

- **Gates:** global incentives make meaningful slowdown unlikely.
- **Plan A:** an international arrangement should buy time and pause at a
  control boundary.
- **Salon task:** separate what is difficult from what is politically
  impossible.

### Openness

- **Plurality:** open collaboration and broad participation can create public
  value.
- **Plan A:** radical research transparency is a security and verification
  mechanism.
- **Salon task:** design levels of access rather than saying "open" or
  "closed."

### Universality and difference

- **Human-rights approach:** every person has an equal moral floor.
- **Plurality approach:** cooperation must preserve and use social difference.
- **Salon task:** name one universal protection and three legitimate local
  variations.

## Facilitation cautions

- Do not ask a participant to speak for a religion, country, identity, or
  "the Global South."
- Do not let capability forecasts consume the entire event. Ask what should be
  done under several timelines.
- Do not treat consensus as the only successful output.
- When a participant says "we," ask which institution and which people they
  mean.
- When someone proposes a human in the loop, ask what authority, time,
  information, and protection that person has.
- When someone invokes innovation, ask who defines progress and receives the
  benefit.

## Take-home card

> Before an AI-mediated decision, ask:
>
> 1. What human good are we pursuing?
> 2. Who chose that end?
> 3. Who cannot refuse?
> 4. Who benefits and who absorbs error?
> 5. How can the decision be contested and repaired?
> 6. What remains meaningfully human?
> 7. When will we stop or reconsider?
