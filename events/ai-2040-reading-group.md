# AI 2040: Plan A - meeting brief

**Prepared for:** Tuesday, September 1, 2026
**Use:** a compact intervention that complements the supplied reading-group
handout rather than repeating it

## The point to carry into the room

Plan A's most durable contribution may not be its preferred treaty. It is the
demand that AI policy survive a dated causal story. Its central weakness is
that technical verifiability cannot substitute for political legitimacy:
humanity remains larger than the actors who own frontier compute or negotiate
a US-China bargain.

## A 90-second opening

> I want to preserve the distinction the authors insist on: Plan A is a
> recommendation expressed as a scenario, not their base-rate prediction. That
> lets us ask a more useful question than "Will this happen?" We can ask what
> would have to become true for it to work.
>
> The plan has four unusually concrete virtues: it tries to buy time, make
> frontier activity visible, diffuse capability beyond a single winner, and
> keep choices reversible. Those are serious responses to both loss of control
> and concentration of power.
>
> But the bargain is not yet the polity. Verification devices do not tell us
> who legitimately sets the threshold, whose security counts, what smaller
> states may refuse, or how people contest the permit regime that follows.
> And the 2040 handoff creates a final paradox: a plan built to preserve human
> control culminates in delegating the future to systems said to be worthy of
> trust.
>
> So my crux is this: can Plan A become not only verifiable between great
> powers, but legitimate to the people and countries asked to live under it?

## Source hygiene

- [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/) describes itself as a concrete predictive
  scenario and says its goal is predictive accuracy. It also states that 2027
  was the authors' modal year at publication, not a certainty, and points to
  later forecasts.
- [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/) says explicitly that Plan A is
  primarily a **recommendation**, not a prediction. The depicted downstream
  effects are predictions conditional on implementation.
- The [reading-group handout](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JQRiPaIs8ouB9kUVEs-ht--jgEzxv-ty/edit)
  usefully separates timelines, the deal, rival plans, failure modes, and
  forecasts.

Do not allow a discussion participant - including the facilitator - to turn a
scenario date into an observed fact.

## Four source checks worth putting on one slide

1. **10-30% is not a house interval.** The Plan A text says individual
   opinions within the team vary between 10% and 30% on literal AI-driven
   human extinction. The handout's compressed wording should not imply one
   shared team estimate.
2. **2030 is a scenario premise, not an independent model output.** The
   [takeoff supplement](https://ai-2040.com/print/takeoff-forecast) says some
   parameters were adjusted while the narrative and model were written
   together; one parameter was set to place the automated-coder milestone in
   January 2030.
3. **The treaty has a modeled durability problem.** The
   [deal-decline supplement](https://ai-2040.com/print/deal-decline) estimates
   an inside-view cumulative decline probability of about 37% within five
   years and 48% within ten. Its adjusted base-rate analysis implies about 62%
   cumulative breakdown by 2040, while the unadjusted base rate implies about
   79%. The figures were retrieved August 30, 2026, and the author emphasizes
   substantial uncertainty.
4. **The alignment roadmap is not yet Plan A's complete roadmap.** Its own
   disclaimer calls it a rough draft focused almost entirely on Plan C. This
   makes the 2038 alignment story a research dependency, not an implementation
   plan.

These points do not dispose of Plan A. They demonstrate the source's own method:
make assumptions visible enough to challenge.

## Six crux cards

### 1. Timeline: is automated AI R&D a date or a causal threshold?

**Plan A dependency:** without intervention, fully automated AI R&D would
arrive around 2030 and rapidly lead to superintelligence.

**Ask**

- What measurable capability counts as "fully automated"?
- Does automation require replacing the whole workflow or only accelerating
  its bottleneck?
- Which 2027 observation would move the median by at least two years?

**Strong answer:** names a benchmark, organizational condition, and update
rule - not only a date.

### 2. Verification: is visible compute a sufficient proxy for dangerous work?

**Plan A dependency:** chip supply chains, declared compute, monitored
datacenters, and inference-only verification make prohibited training
detectable.

**Ask**

- What happens if algorithmic efficiency shifts the capability/compute
  relationship?
- Which hidden activity is easier: undeclared compute, distributed training,
  prohibited fine-tuning, or research performed within permitted inference?
- Who certifies the certifiers?

**Strong answer:** specifies false-positive and false-negative tolerance plus
what happens after an ambiguous signal.

### 3. Transparency: when does openness reduce danger, and when does it diffuse
dangerous capability?

**Plan A dependency:** total AI R&D transparency reduces the incentive to hoard
algorithmic secrets and helps multiple actors catch up.

**Ask**

- Which knowledge must be shared with treaty auditors, peer labs, all
  governments, or the public?
- How are security-relevant details separated from evidence necessary for
  accountability?
- Does transparency reduce concentration if only well-resourced actors can use
  what is disclosed?

**Strong answer:** treats transparency as a tiered institutional design, not a
binary virtue.

### 4. Bargaining: why would the parties sign and continue to comply?

**Plan A dependency:** the United States and China each prefer a verified pause
to the risk of the other side reaching superintelligence first.

**Ask**

- What domestic coalition makes ratification and implementation durable?
- What concession makes the bargain acceptable to states outside the two
  principal powers?
- How do Canada, Mongolia, and other proposed hosts exercise agency rather
  than serve as strategic terrain?

**Strong answer:** models incentives inside states and gives third countries
decision rights.

### 5. Pause boundary: does "top-human-expert level" create a stable control
regime?

**Plan A dependency:** control-based safety remains adequate below a capability
boundary and alignment science matures during the pause.

**Ask**

- What evidence shows that control degrades at a coherent boundary?
- Who can halt deployment when commercial or security pressure rises?
- What happens if alignment science does not mature on schedule?

**Strong answer:** defines a conservative release gate and an indefinite-pause
fallback.

### 6. Legitimacy: can humanity retain authorship after the "handoff"?

**Plan A dependency:** by 2040, superintelligent systems can be trusted enough
to receive extraordinary responsibility.

**Ask**

- What power is handed off: advice, implementation, agenda setting, coercion,
  constitutional amendment, or all of these?
- Which human decisions remain non-delegable even if machine judgment appears
  better?
- How can future people withdraw consent from the settlement?

**Strong answer:** defines reserved human authority, continuing contestability,
and constitutional review.

## DAPPR stress test

The following is this project's synthesis, not the authors' self-assessment.

| Lens | Plan A strength | Unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dignity | Seeks survival and a flourishing future | Does not fully state rights or human activities that may never be optimized away |
| Agency | Makes continued human control a central goal | Great-power negotiators and eventual AI handoff may narrow ordinary human agency |
| Plurality | Diffuses frontier capability among more firms and countries | Diffusion of capability is not the same as participation by diverse publics |
| Power | Directly confronts lab and winner-take-all concentration | Auditors, permit issuers, and compute governors become a new constitutional center |
| Reversibility | Buy time, pause, verification, and reversibility are explicit strengths | Mutually assured compute destruction is itself escalatory and may be hard to reverse safely |

## Two high-value interventions

### Steal the method, not necessarily the conclusion

Scenario scrutiny should be applied to every rival plan and to ordinary
incremental policy. Write the successful dated pathway; then find the first
sentence where "and then" conceals a missing institution.

### Treat legitimacy as part of safety

A technically enforceable arrangement that states or publics experience as
domination creates incentives to evade, sabotage, or exit. Representation,
appeal, burden sharing, and equitable technical capacity are not decorative
ethics added after verification. They are conditions of durable compliance.

## Forecast discipline for the final round

For each percentage:

1. define the event precisely;
2. state a base rate or nearest analogy;
3. name the strongest piece of current evidence;
4. name one observation that would halve the probability;
5. record the date and retain the original number.

For the handout's US-China agreement question, distinguish an announced
agreement from one that is verified, covers relevant compute, and survives a
material compliance dispute.

## Questions worth saving for networking

- If Plan A's adoption probability is low, which component has the highest
  independent value now?
- What should a city, university, or civil-society network build while states
  negotiate?
- Can total research transparency coexist with security and the right not to
  have one's data or knowledge absorbed?
- What would a Plurality version of Plan A's negotiating process look like?
- Which activity would you designate Human Reserved during the 2035 pause?
- What constitutional authority should no AI system receive, even after a
  persuasive safety case?

## Closing line

> A pause buys time only if institutions use the time to become more
> legitimate, more capable, and more accountable. Otherwise, delay simply
> preserves the same concentration of power for a later handoff.
